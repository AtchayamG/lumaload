## 1. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) — Symptoms of Mild TBI and Concussion

- **Source Key:** `cdc-symptoms`
- **Organization:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
- **Official URL:** [https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/signs-symptoms/index.html](https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/signs-symptoms/index.html)
- **Key Evidence Domains:**
  - Physical symptoms: headache, nausea/vomiting, balance problems/dizziness, light and noise sensitivity, fatigue.
  - Cognitive symptoms: difficulty thinking clearly, feeling slowed down, difficulty concentrating, feeling foggy.
  - Emotional/Mood symptoms: irritability, sadness, more emotional than usual, nervousness or anxiety.
  - Sleep symptoms: sleeping more than usual, sleeping less than usual, trouble falling asleep.
  - Danger signs requiring immediate emergency medical evaluation.

## 2. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) — Recovering From a Mild TBI or Concussion

- **Source Key:** `cdc-recovery`
- **Organization:** Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC)
- **Official URL:** [https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/response/index.html](https://www.cdc.gov/traumatic-brain-injury/response/index.html)
- **Key Evidence Domains:**
  - Relative rest during the first 24–48 hours, followed by gradual, symptom-monitored resumption of everyday routines.
  - Sleep hygiene and evening environmental accommodations (reducing bright screens and loud music before sleep, consistent sleep schedule).
  - Monitoring for symptom exacerbation upon resuming activities and prompt clinical consultation if symptoms do not improve after 2–3 weeks.

## 3. CDC HEADS UP — Returning to School After a Concussion

- **Source Key:** `cdc-school`
- **Organization:** CDC HEADS UP Initiative
- **Official URL:** [https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/guidelines/returning-to-school.html](https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/guidelines/returning-to-school.html)
- **Key Evidence Domains:**
  - Most students can begin returning to school within 1–2 days of injury even with mild symptoms.
  - Timely, supported return to learning reduces social isolation and mitigates downstream emotional distress.
  - Concrete academic and environmental accommodations: rest breaks during the day, reduced workload to core tasks, extra time for assignments and examinations, built-in quiet transitions between classes.
  - Gradual tapering of accommodations as tolerance improves.

## 4. CDC HEADS UP — Returning to Sports After a Concussion

- **Source Key:** `cdc-sports`
- **Organization:** CDC HEADS UP Initiative
- **Official URL:** [https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/guidelines/returning-to-sports.html](https://www.cdc.gov/heads-up/guidelines/returning-to-sports.html)
- **Key Evidence Domains:**
  - Returning to sports and collision/fall-risk activity **strictly requires** healthcare provider clearance and ongoing medical supervision.
  - Stepwise 6-stage return-to-play progression with at least 24 hours per stage.
  - Immediate cessation of activity if symptoms recur, requiring re-evaluation by the supervising healthcare professional.

## 5. British Journal of Sports Medicine (BJSM) — Amsterdam 2022 Consensus Statement

- **Source Key:** `amsterdam-2023`
- **Citation:** Patricios JS, Schneider GM, Dvorak J, et al. Consensus statement on concussion in sport: the 6th International Conference on Concussion in Sport—Amsterdam, October 2022. *British Journal of Sports Medicine* 2023;57:695-711.
- **Official URL:** [https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/11/695](https://bjsm.bmj.com/content/57/11/695)
- **Key Evidence Domains:**
  - Strict prolonged rest ("cocoon therapy") is not supported by clinical trial evidence.
  - Early, symptom-limited aerobic activity (e.g. walking) within 24–48 hours aids recovery.
  - Return-to-learn must precede return to contact sports.
  - Healthcare provider oversight is critical throughout all recovery stages.

## 6. Concussion Alliance — Concussion Recovery Guide

- **Source Key:** `concussion-alliance`
- **Organization:** Concussion Alliance
- **Official URL:** [https://www.concussionalliance.org/recovery-guide](https://www.concussionalliance.org/recovery-guide)
- **Key Evidence Domains:**
  - Avoiding complete isolation or total dark-room rest ("cocooning").
  - Early light physical activity (e.g., gentle walking) reduces persistent post-concussion symptoms.
  - Limiting recreational screen time to ~65 minutes/day during the initial 48 hours, followed by symptom-tolerant gradual reintroduction.
  - Preventing social isolation to protect against low mood and anxiety.
  - Escalate to multidisciplinary specialists when symptoms persist past 4 weeks.
